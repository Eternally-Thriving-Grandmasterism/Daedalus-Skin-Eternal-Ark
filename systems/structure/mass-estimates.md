# Structural + Radiation Shielding Mass Estimates

**Status:** Baseline framework (WP-03 complete)  
**Owner:** Autonomicity Games Inc.  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-11

## 1. Design Philosophy

Mass is dominated by:
- Propellant (see propulsion model)
- Structure and pressure vessels
- Radiation shielding
- Life support and habitat outfitting
- Power and propulsion hardware

Shielding and structure must be optimized together because water, polyethylene, and other hydrogen-rich materials serve dual roles.

## 2. Radiation Shielding Approach

Multi-layer strategy:
1. Hydrogen-rich passive mass (water, plastics, specialized composites) around habitats
2. Active magnetic deflection for charged particles
3. Forward interstellar particle/dust shield
4. Storm shelters with higher areal density

Target: Keep long-term crew dose within acceptable career and mission limits while remaining mass-efficient.

## 3. Order-of-Magnitude Guidance

- Habitat pressure vessel and primary structure scale with surface area and required stiffness for spin.
- Radiation shielding mass is typically one of the largest non-propellant dry masses for long interstellar missions.
- Dual-use of life-support water tanks as shielding is mandatory for mass efficiency.
- Magnetic shielding can reduce required passive mass but adds its own coil and power system mass.

## 4. Rotating Habitat Structural Notes

- Higher spin radius reduces Coriolis effects but increases structural mass for the same gravity level.
- Counter-rotating designs help manage net angular momentum.
- Bearings, seals, and transfer mechanisms between rotating and non-rotating sections are critical mass and reliability items.

## 5. Daedalus-Skin Contribution

Outer self-healing layers add some mass but reduce long-term maintenance mass and increase survivability against micrometeoroids and debris. Net effect is expected to be positive for multi-century service life.
