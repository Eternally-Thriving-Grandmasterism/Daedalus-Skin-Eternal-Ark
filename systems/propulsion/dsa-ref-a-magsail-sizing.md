# DSA-Ref-A Magnetic Sail — Parametric Sizing

**Status:** Planning baseline  
**Reference Design:** DSA-Ref-A  
**Target cruise:** ~0.055 c  
**Owner:** Autonomicity Games Inc.  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-11

## 1. Purpose

Provide the primary means of deceleration from interstellar cruise so that only modest residual fusion propellant is required.

## 2. Key Performance Drivers

Magnetic sail drag depends on:
- Magnetic moment (or effective field volume)
- Local interstellar plasma density and velocity relative to the ship
- Sail orientation and stability

Interstellar medium density is low and variable; therefore deceleration times are measured in years even with large sails.

## 3. Design Guidance for DSA-Ref-A

- The sail must be sized so that deceleration from ~0.055 c to interplanetary speeds occurs in an acceptable mission time (target: years to low tens of years, not centuries).
- Larger magnetic moment improves performance but increases mass, stowage volume, deployment complexity, and structural demands.
- Baseline approach: large deployable superconducting loop (or array) stowed along the non-rotating spine during boost and coast, then deployed for deceleration.

## 4. Current Planning Stance

Until higher-fidelity plasma interaction models are available, the Reference Seed Vessel carries:

- A magnetic sail system whose mass is allocated in the 200–800 t range (see mass budget)
- Deployment and attitude control systems with high reliability
- Ability to use residual fusion thrust to augment or finish deceleration if sail performance is lower than hoped

## 5. Critical Open Work

- Quantitative drag models vs. magnetic moment and plasma density
- Structural design of very large lightweight superconducting structures
- Deployment dynamics and failure recovery
- Interaction with the forward particle shield and residual thrust plumes
