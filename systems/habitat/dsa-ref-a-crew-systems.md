# DSA-Ref-A — Crew Systems, Psychological & Social Design

**Status:** Baseline design guidance  
**Reference Population:** ~300  
**Owner:** Autonomicity Games Inc.  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-11

## 1. Purpose

Ensure that the technical architecture is matched by a human systems design capable of supporting a small closed population for multi-year to multi-decade missions without social or psychological collapse.

## 2. Core Design Principles

- **Variety of environment** — Visual, spatial, and functional diversity inside the habitat
- **Private and public balance** — Adequate private space plus strong communal areas
- **Meaningful work and agency** — Crew must have real responsibility and influence
- **Conflict resolution pathways** — Clear, fair, and accessible processes
- **Connection to mission purpose** — Continuous reinforcement of the larger meaning of the voyage
- **PATSAGi / human partnership** — AI support for monitoring and mediation while preserving human sovereignty

## 3. Spatial & Architectural Provisions

- Private quarters sized for psychological sustainability, not minimum survival
- Multiple distinct social zones (quiet, active, cultural, recreational)
- Green spaces and living biological areas integrated with life support
- Windows or high-quality exterior visualization where structurally feasible
- Clear circulation and wayfinding to reduce monotony and disorientation

## 4. Social & Governance Provisions

- Transparent decision processes for internal community matters
- Defined escalation path to ship command and, ultimately, to human authority under TOLC 8
- Training in conflict resolution, cultural mediation, and long-duration isolation effects
- Rotation of roles where practical to maintain engagement
- Continuous but non-intrusive mental health monitoring with strong privacy protections

## 5. Selection & Training

- Crew selection emphasizes psychological resilience, social skills, and systems thinking in addition to technical competence
- Extended multi-month to multi-year simulation campaigns before flight
- Family and relationship considerations explicitly addressed for long missions

## 6. Risk Linkage

Social and psychological failure remains a high-impact risk (R-10). Habitat design, selection, training, and PATSAGi-supported mediation are the primary mitigations. These must be treated with the same seriousness as propulsion or life support.

---

**Technical success is necessary but not sufficient. The human system must also be designed to thrive.**
