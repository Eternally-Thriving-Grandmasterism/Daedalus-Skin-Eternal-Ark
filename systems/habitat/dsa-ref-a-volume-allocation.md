# DSA-Ref-A — Detailed Habitat Volume Allocation

**Status:** Planning baseline  
**Reference Population:** ~300  
**Spin Radius:** ~140 m  
**Owner:** Autonomicity Games Inc.  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-11

## 1. Purpose

Translate the high-level habitat concept into a first quantitative volume allocation so that structural, life-support, and crew-systems design can be cross-checked against real spatial requirements.

## 2. Reference Geometry Assumptions

- Spin radius to main living deck: 140 m
- Approximate cylindrical (or short toroidal) living volume
- Multiple deck levels within the rotating section
- Non-rotating spine for propulsion, power, docking, industry, and zero-g research

## 3. Volume Allocation Categories (300 people)

| Category | Planning Volume Guidance | Notes |
|----------|--------------------------|-------|
| Private quarters | 25–40 m³ per person | Psychological sustainability over multi-year missions |
| Communal / social / cultural | 8–15 m³ per person | Dining, gathering, recreation, culture |
| Food production (plants + access) | 2,000–3,500 m² cultivated area → significant volume | From life-support tables; height allowance required |
| Algae / photobioreactor systems | Dedicated technical volume | High productivity, lower human-accessible space |
| Medical & health | 200–400 m³ total | Clinic, isolation, exercise, mental health |
| Workshops / manufacturing / maintenance | 500–1,200 m³ | On-board repair and fabrication |
| Storage (food contingency, spares, personal) | 400–800 m³ | |
| Circulation, utilities, structure | 15–25% of total pressurized volume | Corridors, shafts, systems distribution |
| Green / park / restorative space | Explicit allocation required | Critical for long-duration morale |

## 4. Derived Planning Implications

- Private + communal space alone for 300 people already implies a large pressurized volume before food production and technical systems are added.
- Food production area is a major volume driver and must be integrated with the rotating habitat rather than treated as an afterthought.
- The ~140 m radius provides sufficient circumference and deck area, but axial length and number of decks must be sized carefully to avoid excessive structural mass.

## 5. Design Rules

- Do not optimize private volume down to bare survival levels; psychological sustainability is a safety requirement.
- Maintain clear separation between dirty/technical zones and living/social zones.
- Provide multiple independent social nodes so that a single conflict or contamination event cannot isolate the entire community.
- Ensure emergency assembly and shelter volumes are reachable under degraded conditions.

## 6. Next Refinement Steps

- Produce a deck-by-deck layout concept with approximate areas
- Cross-check total pressurized volume against the structural mass model
- Integrate life-support equipment footprints and water storage (shielding dual-use)
- Define minimum acceptable private volume after human-factors review
