# Industrial Capacity & In-Space Manufacturing Requirements

**Status:** First analysis  
**Reference Design:** DSA-Ref-A  
**Owner:** Autonomicity Games Inc.  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-11

## 1. Purpose

Assess what industrial and in-space capabilities must exist before the Reference Seed Vessel can be realistically assembled and flown.

## 2. Key Capability Requirements

### A. Launch & Delivery
- Ability to deliver very large structural elements, tanks, and modules to cislunar or high solar orbit
- Either heavy-lift launch cadence at unprecedented scale or substantial in-space manufacturing to reduce launch mass

### B. Orbital Construction Infrastructure
- Large-scale robotic and/or teleoperated assembly capability
- High-power, high-thermal-rejection construction platform(s)
- Precision alignment, joining (welding, mechanical, adhesive), and inspection systems for large structures
- Rotating-interface assembly and verification capability

### C. Materials & Components
- Space-qualified high-specific-strength structural materials at the required scale
- Reliable long-life rotating seals and bearings
- Superconducting systems suitable for magnetic nozzle and (if used) magnetic sail
- Closed-loop life support hardware at multi-hundred-person scale

### D. Fuel Infrastructure
- Demonstrated ³He acquisition pathway (lunar and/or gas-giant) at the required rate
- Deuterium supply chain
- Propellant depot or direct loading capability in the assembly orbit

### E. Quality, Safety & Governance
- Configuration control and traceability across a multi-year orbital construction project
- Independent verification and PATSAGi / TOLC 8 review gates
- Crew training and long-duration simulation facilities synchronized with the physical vehicle

## 3. Strategic Implications

- DSA-Ref-A is deliberately smaller than a full Worldship, but it still demands industrial capacity far beyond current human spaceflight infrastructure.
- The critical path is likely dominated by: (1) fusion drive endurance, (2) large rotating habitat mechanics, (3) closed-loop life support stability, and (4) orbital construction capability.
- A progressive approach (technology maturation → subscale demonstrations → Seed Vessel) is mandatory. Attempting to jump directly to full assembly without these capabilities would be high-risk under TOLC 8.

## 4. Recommended Program Stance

- Treat orbital construction infrastructure and fuel acquisition as parallel programs to the ship design itself.
- Keep the Seed Vessel as conservative as possible in scale and performance to pull the required industrial capabilities into existence rather than assuming they already exist.
- Use the technology maturation plan as the gate for when industrial investment at full Seed Vessel scale is justified.
